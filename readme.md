The advent of Integrated Sensors and Communications (ISAC) marks a transformative breakthrough in the field of 6G network technology. While radar sensors and wireless communication have traditionally developed independently, the emergence of integrated communication and sensing has introduced a paradigm shift, offering unprecedented opportunities for revolutionary spectrum efficiency, reduced hardware costs and power consumption, and redefining ways of network perception and interaction with the surrounding environment. Against this backdrop, ISAC has attracted significant research interest and attention from both academic and industrial sectors. Indeed, various scenarios and challenges have been explored. These include dual-function radar and communication (DFRC), unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), waveform design, beamforming design, security, [6] and smart reflective surfaces, [7][8].
The research field of ISAC, centered on communication, is a promising area. It leverages optimized communication waveforms for sensing tasks, offering the potential to enhance perception capabilities through the use of transmitted communication signals that provide effective signal-of-interest (SoI) enhancements. The redesign of the process for estimating channel state information (CSI) provides an opportunity to precisely determine physical sensing parameters such as the arrival direction (DoA), range, and velocity of interest targets. Indeed, numerous studies have examined and validated the combination of dual-base station radar topologies with OFDM-based signal processing to optimize communication-aided sensing [10]-[12]. Notably, they explored this approach in actual dual-base station settings, demonstrating its effectiveness in accurately detecting and estimating target parameters such as delay and Doppler shifts. Additionally, in [14], a power budget analysis was conducted to study the behavior of communication and radar Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) with distance variation. However, achieving high sensing quality in practice remains challenging due to the lack of mutual optimization between communication and radar systems for sensing functions. Challenges arise when designing radar signal processing features, including peak-to-average power ratios [15], clutter noise, multipath reflections, unoptimized sidelobes, and Doppler ambiguities caused by cyclic prefixes (CPs) [16].
In this work, we investigate the parameter estimation within a dual-baseline radar setup within an ISAC system, focusing on estimating the Angle of Arrival (AoA) and Angle of Departure (AoD). Since maximum likelihood estimation is a highly multidimensional optimization problem that is not feasible for real-time implementation with actual hardware resources, we propose to leverage the capabilities of neural networks (NN) for generalized approximations. We execute this complex estimation problem with low complexity optimization NN architectures. In literature [17], the AoA and AoD estimation problems are either framed as regression problems [18], [19] whereby training architectures such as Multilayer Perceptrons (MLP) are trained to minimize the objective function, such as Mean Square Error (MSE), between estimated and true angles, or as spectral-based sensing estimation [20], [21] where NNs are trained on thermally encoded ground truth vectors representing discrete grids with a given base number, representing the likelihood of targets at given angles. In the latter case, the network does not rely on prior knowledge of target counts. Instead, it is trained to output the spectrum of estimated angles, which then requires additional steps for target detection, such as peak extraction angle estimates, as typically done in conventional methods (e.g., MUSIC). Other DL methods have been proposed, such as multi-task autoencoders and a series of parallel multi-layer classifiers [22]. In our study, we propose a new method for sensor estimation using different inputs and train a complex value NN to minimize the MSE objective function on the training dataset generated using an ideal system model. Additionally, we propose a parameterized algorithm that utilizes knowledge-based models for benchmark testing. More specifically, the parameterized 2D method utilizes full knowledge of array steering vectors and OFDM subcarrier rule structures to estimate target AoA and AoD. Although OFDM is assumed in the model, our work remains effective for any pre-coded OFDM modulation, such as Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) extended OFDM. To compare, the Cram 'er-Rao Bound (CRB) is also shown. The results indicate that the DL solution provides comparable estimation performance to the parameterized approach while requiring significantly less computational effort.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
1.1 A novel joint AoA/AoD DL estimator. We propose a new DL technique for parameter estimation in the ISAC system, namely AoA and AoD. Starting from traditional input sources, this method utilizes an estimated channel matrix, capturing essential information about the sensed parameters. The DL model employs a complex-valued neural network, including complex convolutional layers and linear layers, with weights represented as complex matrices, enhancing the model's ability to accurately represent the channel matrix. The activation function is chosen as the complex-valued relu (CReLU). The network structure includes three hidden layers and a final output layer for predicting the sensed parameters. Additionally, MSE is used as the objective function during training.
1.2 A novel design for parameterized AoA/AoD estimators. We develop a parameterized approach for AoA/AoD estimation, leveraging complete model knowledge, including the geometric shape of the array. This method involves transforming the received channel matrix into a smaller one with given subarray size by utilizing uniform linear antenna (ULA) structures on both the transmitter and receiver sides. It further computes certain eigenvalues using induced Hankel structures, which aids in AoD estimation. For AoA estimation, least squares (LS) fitting is required at a later stage of the algorithm.

Conclusion This paper proposes two joint AoA and AoD estimation methods for a dual-baseline ISAC system. One solution is based on DL, which utilizes complex valued NNs and incorporates preprocessing steps involving rough timing estimation to reduce input size and improve computational efficiency. The second method is a parametric solution that employs model knowledge as a benchmark. Compared to the parametric approach, the DL-based method demonstrates competitive performance while requiring lower online computation complexity in terms of the number of additions and multiplications. Moreover, the DL algorithm can accurately estimate the number of targets with the same discrete time delay, making it suitable for dynamic environments. Despite this, while the performance gap between the DL method and the benchmark method is generally acceptable, further research and optimization of complex NNs are needed to enhance performance, especially under high SNR conditions.
It is worth noting that the existing implementations of DL techniques for 2D AoA and AoD estimation and the parametric method make it necessary to re-execute these methods whenever new target estimations are required. Recognizing this limitation, our upcoming research will focus on the development of tracking mechanisms within both DL and parametric methods. This intentional integration is designed to simplify the process of updating AoA and AoD estimates, eliminating the need for re-estimation of sensing parameters, particularly in dynamic scenarios.
